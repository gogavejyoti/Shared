When "More Productive" Might Mean "Less Capacity"

This report looks at six months of contact centre activity for January to June 2026, covering three business areas (CX, CS and CX International) across voice, chat and email, plus twelve weeks of scheduling data (Wk14 to Wk25) on adherence, shift conformance and scheduled headcount. The headline finding is uncomfortable: the operation looks steadily more efficient on paper, but that improvement is driven mainly by delivered hours falling far faster than the work coming in. An interactive dashboard was built alongside this analysis, showing monthly volume against delivered hours, productivity versus handling time, channel mix, weekly adherence and conformance, headcount versus performance, and a weekly operational risk score.

In Summary

Volume is softening, but delivered hours are falling three times faster, and that mismatch — not demand — is what is driving the apparent 73% productivity gain. June stands out as an anomaly on every measure at once and should be validated before it anchors any forecast. Meanwhile the work mix is shifting: voice is shrinking and volatile, chat is growing, CS email has halved while CX International email has grown 71%, and scheduling conformance remains erratic, particularly in CX. Taken together, the picture is of an operation whose reported efficiency is improving while its measured capacity and scheduling discipline are becoming less reliable — a combination that would be tested quickly if volume returned to April levels.
